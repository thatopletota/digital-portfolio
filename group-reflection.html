The Art of Working Together

A reflection on teamwork, communication, conflict, responsibility, and the lessons that help a group grow.

Group Work Reflection • Tuckman's Model • 2026

From Individual Contributions to Collective Achievement

Group work is more than dividing tasks among several people. It requires individuals with different personalities, ideas, strengths, and working habits to cooperate towards a common objective. Reflecting on teamwork through Bruce Tuckman's model has helped me understand that effective collaboration develops over time. A group does not automatically become successful simply because its members have been assigned to work together; it must build trust, establish expectations, navigate differences, and learn to take shared responsibility.

Stage 01 · Getting Started

1. Forming — Establishing Direction

The forming stage is when group members first come together, become familiar with one another, and begin to understand the task. At this point, members may be polite and enthusiastic, but they might also feel uncertain about expectations, responsibilities, or how best to contribute.

This stage highlights the importance of establishing a clear foundation. A group benefits from discussing the objectives, identifying individual strengths, agreeing on communication methods, and assigning responsibilities fairly. Without this initial organisation, members may develop different interpretations of the task.

My learning: I recognise that listening carefully, asking questions, and understanding expectations are important first steps. I want to contribute to creating an environment where everyone understands the goal and feels comfortable participating.

Stage 02 · Facing Differences

2. Storming — Managing Challenges

During the storming stage, differences in opinions, personalities, priorities, and working styles may become more noticeable. Members might disagree about how to approach a task, how to distribute the workload, or how quickly decisions should be made. These disagreements can create tension if they are not addressed constructively.

However, disagreement is not necessarily evidence that a group is failing. It can reveal weaknesses in the original plan and introduce perspectives that the group had not previously considered. The important distinction is between challenging an idea respectfully and making a disagreement personal. Active listening, clear communication, and a willingness to compromise can help a group move forward.

My learning: I want to become more confident in expressing my views while remaining open to the ideas of others. Instead of avoiding difficult conversations, I can approach them calmly, focus on the shared goal, and help find solutions that are fair and practical.

Stage 03 · Building Trust

3. Norming — Developing Cooperation

In the norming stage, group members begin to establish stronger relationships, shared expectations, and more effective ways of working. Roles become clearer, communication may improve, and members increasingly recognise the value of one another's contributions. The group begins to operate with a stronger sense of unity.

This stage demonstrates that trust is built through consistent behaviour. Meeting agreed deadlines, communicating changes early, respecting different viewpoints, and completing assigned responsibilities help members rely on one another. Fairness is also important: cooperation should not mean that a few members carry most of the workload while others remain disengaged.

My learning: I can help build trust by being dependable, keeping others informed, respecting agreed responsibilities, and offering support when appropriate. I also need to recognise that being a good team member involves more than completing my own section; it means considering how my work contributes to the whole group's progress.

Stage 04 · Working Effectively

4. Performing — Achieving Shared Goals

During the performing stage, the group works more effectively towards its objectives. Members understand their roles, communicate more confidently, and focus on completing the task rather than repeatedly negotiating how to work together. The team can use its different strengths to solve problems and improve the quality of its output.

Effective performance still requires planning and accountability. Members should review one another's contributions, check that the work meets the requirements, and address gaps before the final submission. In academic projects, for example, combining separate sections is not enough on its own; the final work should be consistent, accurate, and clearly connected to the overall objective.

My learning: I want to develop my ability to contribute ideas, solve problems, review work critically, and support the group without waiting to be instructed at every step. Taking initiative and remaining accountable can help me become a more valuable collaborator.

Stage 05 · Looking Back

5. Adjourning — Evaluating and Moving Forward

The adjourning stage occurs when the group completes its work and prepares to separate or move on to other responsibilities. It is an opportunity to recognise achievements, acknowledge contributions, and reflect on what could have been done differently. Completing the task is important, but understanding the experience can make future collaboration more effective.

A useful review considers whether the group achieved its objectives, communicated effectively, distributed responsibilities fairly, and managed its time well. It should also identify practical improvements, such as starting earlier, setting clearer deadlines, or checking progress more regularly. Reflection turns a completed task into a source of learning.

My learning: I want to evaluate both the final result and the process used to achieve it. Recognising what worked well and what needs improvement will help me carry useful lessons into future academic projects and professional environments.

My Overall Reflection

Collaboration is a skill that must be developed, not simply expected.

Tuckman's model has helped me understand that group development involves more than reaching the final stage. Each stage presents different needs, from establishing direction to managing disagreements and evaluating the final outcome. I have also learned that effective teamwork requires self-awareness: I must understand my own working habits, communicate responsibly, and recognise how my behaviour affects others.

In future group projects, I intend to clarify expectations early, contribute consistently, communicate concerns respectfully, and take responsibility for my commitments. I also want to be more intentional about listening to other perspectives and giving constructive feedback. These practices can help create a group environment in which members feel respected and the quality of the work remains the shared priority.

Looking Ahead

How I intend to become a stronger team member

  • Communicate early: clarify instructions, responsibilities, and deadlines before work begins.
  • Be accountable: complete assigned tasks on time and inform the group promptly if a challenge arises.
  • Respect differences: listen to alternative viewpoints and address disagreements constructively.
  • Contribute actively: share ideas, ask questions, and offer practical support when needed.
  • Reflect after completion: identify what worked, what did not, and what should change next time.
